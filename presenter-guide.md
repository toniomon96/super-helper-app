# Presenter Guide — Super Helper App
## Career Day at Southridge Elementary School, Lewisville, TX

---

## Live URL

Share this with the teacher or post it in the classroom after Career Day:
**https://southridgeelementarycareerday.com**

The site works on any phone, tablet, or computer — no app install needed.

## Before You Start

- Open `index.html` in a browser and go full screen (F11 or View → Full Screen).
- Test every button at least once before the session.
- Print the concept cards from `printable-cards.html` as a backup.
- Do **not** type student names into the app at any point.

---

## The Core Message

> "Apps are not magic. They are ideas plus instructions."

Everything you say and do in the session should come back to this line. Repeat it at least twice.

---

## Personal Intro Script (3–4 min — use before every session)

*Covers the three things the school asks every presenter to address: job overview, typical day, and skills/education.*

> "Hi everyone — my name is Toni, and I work at Microsoft as an AI Cloud Solutions Engineer.
> That's a mouthful, so here's the simple version: **I help companies use artificial intelligence and cloud technology to solve real problems.**"

*(~1 min)* **Your job and industry:**
> "Microsoft makes software and tools used by billions of people — Windows, Xbox, Teams, and a lot more. My team specifically works on AI — that's Artificial Intelligence — helping businesses figure out how to use it. Think of me as someone who takes a big, complicated technology and helps people actually use it in their everyday work."

*(~1.5 min)* **A typical day:**
> "On a typical day I might:
> — Jump on a video call with a customer who has a problem they need to solve
> — Design a solution using AI tools — basically planning out how the app or system will work
> — Build a demo to show them what it will look like — kind of like what I'm going to show you today
> — And yes, fix bugs. That never goes away, no matter how good you get.
> Every day is different, which is honestly one of my favorite things about it."

*(~1 min)* **Skills and education:**
> "I studied Information Technology in college — that's learning how computers, networks, and software all work together. But here's what I really needed:
> Curiosity — I was always the kid asking 'how does that work?'
> Patience — because when something breaks, you have to stay calm and figure it out.
> And the ability to explain hard things simply — because my job isn't just building things, it's helping people understand them. Which, by the way, is exactly what we're going to do today."

*(~30 sec)* **Transition into the demo:**
> "I actually built an app to show you how this all works. Input. Code. Output. Let's go."

## Opening Script (use for all session lengths)

> "Hey everyone — who here has used an app before? A game, a video, anything on a phone or tablet?"

*(Pause for hands.)*

> "Awesome. So today I build apps — that's part of my job. And I want to show you something: apps are not magic. They are just ideas plus instructions. Let me show you what I mean."

*(Open the Super Helper App on screen.)*

## Session Versions

---

### 10-Minute Version

**Goal:** Leave kids with one idea — Input → Code → Output.

*Note: This version skips the Personal Intro Script. The three topics the school asks for (job overview, typical day, skills/education) are not covered — use this only as an emergency fallback if time runs short.*

1. Ask: "Who here uses apps?"
2. Say the core message: "Apps are not magic. They are ideas plus instructions."
3. Show the app on screen.
4. Ask the class to vote: "Which button should we press?"
5. Click the winning button. Read the output out loud.
6. Explain the flow: "The input was what you chose. The code is the instruction. The output is what popped up."
7. Click **Make a bug**. Show the error. Say: "Uh oh. This is called a bug. It's when the app does something we didn't want."
8. Click **Fix bug**. Say: "Engineers fix bugs every day. That's a big part of the job."
9. Close with: "Technology is something people build to help other people. And anyone can have app ideas — including you."

---

### 20–25-Minute Version

**Goal:** Show the full demo loop and let kids participate in adding a feature.

1. *(1 min)* Ask students what apps or games they use. Let 2–3 students share.
2. *(3–4 min)* Run the **Personal Intro Script** above — covers your job, your typical day, and skills/education (required by the school).
3. *(30 sec)* Say the core message and open the app on screen.
4. *(3 min)* Let the class vote on buttons. Click 2–3 of the six choices. Read outputs out loud.
5. *(2 min)* Click **Show code**. Walk through the snippet: "See this? It says: if the input is 'bored,' show a creative mission. That's it. That's the whole idea."
6. *(2 min)* Click **Make a bug**. Show the error. Ask: "What went wrong?" Let a student guess.
7. *(1 min)* Click **Fix bug**. Explain: "Engineers test, find bugs, fix them, and try again."
8. *(3 min)* Click **Mystery feature**. Ask: "What button should we add to this app?" Take a couple ideas from the class.
9. *(3 min)* Type the winning idea into the Feature Builder. Click **Add feature**. Let a student come up and press the new button.
10. *(2 min)* Close: "That idea just went from your brain to a real button in about 30 seconds. That's what software engineers do — we take ideas and build them."

---

### 40–45-Minute Version

**Goal:** Full demo plus a hands-on paper activity.

Follow the 20–25-minute version for the first half, then:

11. *(1 min)* Hand out the **Design Your Own App** activity sheet (printed from `printable-cards.html`).
12. *(12–15 min)* Students fill in the four boxes:
    - My app is called…
    - My app helps…
    - My app has this button…
    - When you press it…
13. *(5 min)* Ask 2–3 students to share their app ideas. Celebrate every idea.
14. *(2 min)* Close: "Every single app you use started as an idea someone drew on paper. Now you've done that too."

## Suggested Questions to Ask Students

- "Who here has used an app before?"
- "What's your favorite app or game?"
- "What do you think happens when you press a button on a phone?"
- "What is a bug? Have you ever had a book with a typo?"
- "If you could make any app to help someone, what would it do?"
- "What button should we add to this app? Something silly is fine."
- "What kind of problem do you wish someone would build an app to solve?"

## Backup Plan — No Tech Needed

If the projector, browser, or laptop fails, use the printed concept cards and run it manually.

1. Hold up the **INPUT** card. Ask a student: "What do you need right now — are you bored, tired, or do you want a joke?"
2. Hold up the **CODE** card. Say: "My instructions say: if someone is bored, give them a creative mission."
3. Hold up the **OUTPUT** card. Say out loud: "Draw a dog driving a spaceship."
4. Hold up the **BUG** card. Drop something on the floor dramatically. Say: "Uh oh. That's a bug. Something went wrong."
5. Hold up the **FIX** card. Pick it up. Say: "Now it's fixed."

> "That's how apps work. Input → Code → Output. And bugs happen. And we fix them."

This version is genuinely just as effective with younger kids. Keep it energetic and it will land.

## Privacy and Safety Reminder

- Do **not** type any student names into the app at any time.
- Do **not** take photos of students with the app visible.
- The app does not save any data. Everything clears on refresh.
- Keep feature ideas generic: "pizza button," "joke button," "homework helper" — not "Maria's idea" or "Tyler's button."
